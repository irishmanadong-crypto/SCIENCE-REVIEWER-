Grade 9 — First Quarter (Quarter 1)

[Grade 9 Science – Quarter 1)]

[1. Lesson 1: Respiratory and Circulatory Systems Working with the Other Organ Systems Summary: Explains how the respiratory system (inhaling oxygen and exhaling carbon dioxide) and the circulatory system (the heart pumping blood through blood vessels) work together to deliver oxygen and nutrients to every cell in the body. 2. Lesson 2: Heredity: Inheritance and Variation (Non-Mendelian Genetics) Summary: Discusses patterns of inheritance that do not follow Mendel’s simple dominant-recessive laws—such as Incomplete Dominance, Codominance, Multiple Alleles, and Sex-Linked Traits—as well as the structure of DNA (Double Helix and base-pairing rules). 3. Lesson 3: Biodiversity and Evolution Summary: Focuses on the importance of biodiversity (the variety of living organisms) in maintaining ecosystem stability, how population density is measured, and the causes of species extinction such as habitat destruction (deforestation), pollution, and climate change. 4. Lesson 4: Ecosystems: Life Energy (Photosynthesis and Cellular Respiration) Summary: Explains how energy flows through living organisms via Photosynthesis (food production in plants using sunlight inside the chloroplast) and Cellular Respiration (harvesting energy or ATP from food inside the mitochondria). Summary for Lesson 3 (Biodiversity and Evolution) (In case this is the next lesson you need): Title: Biodiversity and Evolution (Importance of Biodiversity and Species Extinction) Biodiversity: Refers to the variety and abundance of species in a given area. The higher the biodiversity, the more stable the ecosystem. Limiting Factors: Environmental factors that prevent a population from growing too large (e.g., lack of food, water, shelter, and disease). Endangered vs. Extinct: A species is endangered when it is at risk of dying out; it is extinct when the last remaining member of that species dies out completely. Causes of Extinction: Habitat destruction, overhunting, pollution, invasive species, and climate change.]

Grade 9 — Ikalawang Markahan (Quarter 2)

[Overview of Lessons in Grade 9 Science – Quarter 2 Lesson 1: Electronic Structure of Matter Lesson 2: Chemical Bonding (Ionic and Covalent Bonding) Lesson 3: The Variety of Carbon Compounds (Organic Chemistry) Lesson 4: The Mole Concept - Q2]

[Summary and Key Information:Main Objective: This lesson explains how electrons are arranged around the nucleus of an atom using the Quantum Mechanical Model, and how this arrangement determines the chemical behavior and properties of an element.Evolution of Atomic Models (Key Milestones):Niels Bohr’s Planetary Model: Proposed that electrons orbit the nucleus in fixed circular paths or energy levels ($n = 1, 2, 3...$). When electrons absorb energy, they jump to a higher energy level (excited state); when they fall back to their original level (ground state), they release energy in the form of light (observed in flame tests).Quantum Mechanical Model (Erwin Schrödinger): Replaced fixed orbits with atomic orbitals—regions of space around the nucleus where there is a high probability of finding an electron.Principal Energy Levels, Sublevels, and Orbitals:Main Energy Levels ($n$): Designated by numbers $1, 2, 3, 4...$ (corresponding to shells $K, L, M, N...$).Sublevels and Maximum Electrons:$s$ (sharp): 1 orbital — holds up to 2 electrons$p$ (principal): 3 orbitals — holds up to 6 electrons$d$ (diffuse): 5 orbitals — holds up to 10 electrons$f$ (fundamental): 7 orbitals — holds up to 14 electronsRules for Electron Configuration:Aufbau Principle: Electrons fill the lowest available energy sublevels first before moving to higher ones (e.g., $1s \rightarrow 2s \rightarrow 2p \rightarrow 3s \rightarrow 3p \rightarrow 4s \rightarrow 3d$).Pauli Exclusion Principle: A single atomic orbital can hold a maximum of two electrons, and they must have opposite spins ($\uparrow\downarrow$).Hund’s Rule: When filling degenerate orbitals (orbitals of equal energy, like the three $p$ orbitals), electrons occupy them singly first with parallel spins before pairing up.Key Concepts in Understanding the Lesson:Valence Electrons: The electrons located in the outermost energy level of an atom. They are responsible for chemical bonding and can be easily identified using the element’s group number (for representative elements) or its electron configuration.Flame Test: Different metallic elements emit distinct flame colors when heated (e.g., Sodium = yellow, Copper = blue-green, Potassium = lilac/violet) because their electrons absorb heat energy and emit specific wavelengths of light as they return to their ground state..]

Grade 9 — Ikatlong Markahan (Quarter 3)

[Overview of Lessons in Grade 9 Science – Quarter 3 Lesson 1: Volcanoes and Volcanic Eruptions Lesson 2: Climate (Factors Affecting Climate and Global Climate Phenomena) Lesson 3: Constellations (Characteristics of Stars and Changing Positions of Constellations) - Q3]

[Summary and Key Information: Main Objective: This lesson explains what volcanoes are, how they are classified based on their shape and activity, the factors that trigger different types of volcanic eruptions, and how volcanic heat is harnessed as geothermal energy. Classification of Volcanoes by Activity (PHIVOLCS): Active Volcanoes: Have a record of eruption within the last 600 years or those that erupted 10,000 years ago based on analyses of their materials (e.g., Mayon, Taal, Kanlaon, Bulusan). Inactive (Dormant) Volcanoes: Have not erupted for the last 10,000 years and their physical form is being changed by agents of weathering and erosion (e.g., Mt. Apo, Mt. Makiling). Types of Volcanoes According to Shape/Structure: Shield Volcano: Broad, gently sloping sides formed by the accumulation of non-viscous, fluid lava (e.g., Mauna Loa in Hawaii). Cinder Cone (Scoria Cone): Steep-sided, small, symmetrical cone built from ejected lava fragments (cinders or tephra) from a single vent (e.g., Mt. Babuyan, Paricutin). Composite Volcano (Stratovolcano): Tall, symmetrical, cone-shaped mountain composed of alternating layers of lava flows, volcanic ash, and cinders (e.g., Mayon Volcano, Mt. Fuji). Factors Affecting Volcanic Eruptions: Magma Temperature: Higher temperature lowers viscosity, allowing magma to flow more easily. Chemical Composition (Silica Content): High silica content makes magma thick and sticky (high viscosity), trapping gases and leading to violent, explosive eruptions. Low silica content produces runny lava (low viscosity) and quiet, effusive eruptions. Amount of Dissolved Gases: More trapped gases (mainly water vapor, carbon dioxide, and sulfur dioxide) build up pressure, resulting in more explosive eruptions. Types of Volcanic Eruptions: Phreatic (Hydrothermal): Steam-driven eruption caused when hot rocks come in contact with groundwater. Phreatomagmatic: Violent eruption resulting from the direct interaction of new magma and water. Strombolian: Periodic weak-to-violent eruptions characterized by fountain lava. Vulcanian: Tall eruption columns that reach up to 20 km high with pyroclastic flow and ashfall tephra (e.g., Paricutin). Plinian: Excessively explosive type of eruption of gas and pyroclastics, creating massive ash clouds (e.g., Mt. Pinatubo in 1991). Key Concepts in Understanding the Lesson: Geothermal Energy: Heat energy stored beneath the Earth's surface, commonly found in volcanic regions (such as the Philippines, which lies along the Pacific Ring of Fire). Geothermal power plants use steam from underground hot water reservoirs to spin turbines and generate electricity. Effects of Volcanic Eruptions: While eruptions pose hazards like pyroclastic flows, lahars, and ashfall, they also provide long-term benefits such as fertile volcanic soil, geothermal energy, and valuable minerals.]

Grade 9 — Ikaapat na Markahan (Quarter 4)

[Overview of Lessons in Grade 9 Science – Quarter 4 Lesson 1: Force and Motion (Uniformly Accelerated Motion and Projectile Motion) Lesson 2: Momentum and Impulse (Conservation of Linear Momentum) Lesson 3: Work, Power, and Energy (Conservation of Mechanical Energy) Lesson 4: Heat, Work, and Efficiency (Thermodynamics and Heat Engines) Lesson 5: Electricity and Magnetism (Power Generation, Transmission, and Distribution)]

[Summary and Key Information:Main Objective: This lesson explains how objects move under constant acceleration along a straight line (Uniformly Accelerated Motion) and along a curved path under the influence of gravity (Projectile Motion).Uniformly Accelerated Motion (UAM):Refers to motion in which an object's velocity changes at a constant rate over time (constant acceleration, $a$).Horizontal Motion: An object speeding up or slowing down along a flat surface (e.g., a car accelerating on a straight highway).Vertical Motion (Free Fall): An object moving strictly under the influence of Earth's gravity, with a constant downward acceleration due to gravity ($g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2$), ignoring air resistance.Key Kinematic Quantities: Displacement ($d$), Initial Velocity ($v_i$), Final Velocity ($v_f$), Acceleration ($a$), and Time ($t$).Projectile Motion:The curved, parabolic path (trajectory) followed by an object (projectile) thrown or launched into the air, acted upon only by gravity after launch.Independence of Motion: Projectile motion is a combination of two independent components occurring simultaneously:Horizontal Component ($x$-axis): Moves at a constant velocity ($a_x = 0$) because no horizontal force acts on it (ignoring air resistance).Vertical Component ($y$-axis): Experiences constant downward acceleration due to gravity ($a_y = -9.8\text{ m/s}^2$), behaving just like a free-falling object.Key Characteristics of a Trajectory (Launched at an Angle):At the maximum height (peak) of its path, the vertical velocity ($v_y$) momentarily becomes $0\text{ m/s}$, while the horizontal velocity ($v_x$) remains constant and acceleration is still $9.8\text{ m/s}^2$ downward.Angle of Release and Range:A $45^\circ$ launch angle produces the maximum horizontal distance (range) on level ground.Complementary angles (angles that add up to $90^\circ$, such as $30^\circ$ and $60^\circ$, or $15^\circ$ and $75^\circ$) achieve the same horizontal range when launched at the same initial speed, though the higher angle reaches a greater maximum height and stays in the air longer.Key Concepts in Understanding the Lesson:Uniform Motion vs. Uniformly Accelerated Motion: In uniform motion, velocity is constant ($a = 0$); in uniformly accelerated motion, velocity changes steadily every second ($a = \text{constant}$).Real-Life Applications: Understanding projectile motion helps athletes optimize launch angles in sports (such as basketball free throws, javelin throw, and long jump) and allows engineers to calculate safe trajectories.]
